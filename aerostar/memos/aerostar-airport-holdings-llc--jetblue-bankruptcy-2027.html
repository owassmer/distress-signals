02 The company, and how the event reaches its revenue

The company and the revenue

Private operator of Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU/LMM), San Juan, Puerto Rico, under a 40-year lease from the Puerto Rico Ports Authority (from Feb 27, 2013). Earns a fixed, CPI-indexed annual contribution from signatory airlines under the Airport Use Agreements (US$76.8M in 2025), plus passenger-driven non-aeronautical revenue (duty free/retail, food and beverage, rental cars, parking and ground transportation) and other aeronautical fees; FY2025 total operating revenue US$219.6M on 6.8M enplanements.

The revenue this memo is about: Passenger-driven non-aeronautical revenue at Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport: concessions (duty free/retail, food & beverage, terminal services), rental car concessions and parking/ground transportation

Owners and counterparties: 60% owned by Grupo Aeroportuario del Sureste (ASUR, NYSE: ASR) through Aeropuerto de Cancún; 40% held by other shareholders. Lessor and revenue-share recipient: Puerto Rico Ports Authority (PRPA).

How the event reaches the revenue

JetBlue is SJU's largest carrier, with 32.7% of SJU domestic enplanements in May–Aug 2026 after Spirit's exit, and Aerostar's largest customer (26% of ASUR Puerto Rico revenues in 2025). A JetBlue bankruptcy in 2027 would reach 2028 in one of two ways. A reorganization leaves a smaller JetBlue SJU schedule running through 2028. A wind-down ends its flights before 2028 begins, as Spirit's second Chapter 11 ended with its last flight on May 2, 2026. Each JetBlue passenger not carried by another airline is one who does not buy at SJU's concessions or use its parking and rental cars, which Aerostar earns per passenger. Aerostar's airline fees are a fixed total annual contribution under the Airport Use Agreements, owed by the airlines as a group, so a smaller JetBlue does not reduce that line (pre-petition JetBlue receivables could be impaired, which is not modeled).

When

The event's window closes Dec 31, 2027 and the case measures calendar 2028, the first full year after it. A filing can come at any time in the window. Schedule cuts usually follow a filing by one to three months, so a filing by mid-2027 has its cuts fully in place through 2028, and a Jul–Dec 2027 filing has them in place for nearly all of it. A wind-down has historically come months after filing (Spirit filed in Aug 2025 and ceased in May 2026), so a cessation within the window means JetBlue flies none of 2028 and rivals' recapture is still building through the year. In the world where JetBlue does not file by end-2027, a filing in 2028 remains possible, ahead of its 2029 maturities, but that world is held at the baseline. Revenue lost within 2028 is not made up later in the period.

Appendix · what the memo rests on

A Findings recorded on the case

inference

As of Nov 23, 2026, JetBlue has not filed. No petition is on federal dockets, and its only 8-K since Oct 1 is the Oct 26 Q3 release. The credit picture has hardened since the case was first saved. The TrueBlue-backed 2031 notes hit 42 cents in late October, and by Nov 11 about 55% of lenders had signed a cooperation pact, which binds them to negotiate as a group. The Nov 13–15 nationwide FAA ground stop hit JetBlue harder than its peers (43%, 49% and 28% of flights canceled), adding to Q4 losses. Management still says it has no plans to file. The Q3 10-Q states liquidity sufficient for at least 12 months, plus $250M of aircraft-backed financing expected in Q4 2026, and the TrueBlue covenants carry a cross-default. That runway makes a filing before mid-2027 less likely than bond prices alone imply, while 2027 cash burn and maturities raise the pressure later in the window. The earlier finding's $750M minimum-liquidity figure is not stated in any record read; the 10-Q confirms only that minimum-liquidity requirements exist. For calendar 2028, the case's period, any filing within the window reaches the full year. The worst world, a Jul–Dec 2027 filing followed by a wind-down, gives a shortfall of about $15.3M, 7.0% of LMM total operating revenue. A reorganization gives about $1.4–1.5M (0.6–0.7%).[4a][4b][3][2b][2a][1d][5][C2][C1][C3]
