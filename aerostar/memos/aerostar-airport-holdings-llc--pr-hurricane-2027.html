02 The company, and how the event reaches its revenue

The company and the revenue

Private operator of Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU/LMM), San Juan, Puerto Rico, under a 40-year lease from the Puerto Rico Ports Authority (from Feb 27, 2013). Earns a fixed, CPI-indexed annual contribution from signatory airlines under the Airport Use Agreements (US$76.8M in 2025), plus passenger-driven non-aeronautical revenue (duty free/retail, food and beverage, rental cars, parking and ground transportation) and other aeronautical fees; FY2025 total operating revenue US$219.6M on 6.8M enplanements.

The revenue this memo is about: Passenger-driven non-aeronautical revenue at Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport: concessions (duty free/retail, food & beverage, terminal services), rental car concessions and parking/ground transportation

Owners and counterparties: 60% owned by Grupo Aeroportuario del Sureste (ASUR, NYSE: ASR) through Aeropuerto de Cancún; 40% held by other shareholders. Lessor and revenue-share recipient: Puerto Rico Ports Authority (PRPA).

How the event reaches the revenue

A hurricane striking Puerto Rico closes SJU for days and knocks out power on the island. A major storm also suppresses tourist and visiting-friends-and-relatives travel for months. Passengers who do not fly do not buy at SJU's concessions or use its parking and rental cars, which Aerostar earns per passenger. The airline fees are a fixed annual contribution under the Airport Use Agreements and do not fall with traffic. ASUR states it has no insurance for the resulting business interruption (the LMM Lease requires Aerostar to carry such cover), and any recovery would be other income, not revenue.

When

The window is the 2027 Atlantic hurricane season, Jun 1–Nov 30, peaking Aug–Oct. After Maria (Sep 20, 2017), SJU traffic stayed 12–31% below the prior year for about eight months. After Fiona (Sep 18, 2022) the airport closed for about two days. The period runs Jun 1, 2027–May 31, 2028, capturing most of a season strike's aftermath.

What establishes this

  • Seasonal timing of the hurricane exposure[1e]
  • The channel is passenger traffic[1f]
  • Lost passenger revenue is not offset by business-interruption cover, per ASUR[1g]
  • Airline fees are a fixed annual contribution, so the exposed stream is the passenger-driven commercial revenue[1h]

03 The event, and the ways it can turn out

Hurricane strikes Puerto Rico in 2027 season
Decided · June 1 – November 30, 2027

During the 2027 Atlantic hurricane season (June 1 to November 30, 2027), the center of a tropical cyclone at hurricane intensity (maximum sustained winds of 64 kt or more) makes landfall on the main island of Puerto Rico or passes within 50 nautical miles of its coast, as recorded in the National Hurricane Center's best-track data and Tropical Cyclone Reports.

Decided by nature and recorded by NOAA's National Hurricane Center. Recent strikes and close passes include Hugo (1989), Georges (1998), Irma and Maria (2017) and Fiona (2022); seasonal forecasts (CSU, NOAA) issued from April to August 2027 will bear on it. Season Jun 1–Nov 30, 2027; peak mid-Aug to mid-Oct. Final best-track reports usually follow within months of the season.[1a][1b]

Shown byNOAA National Hurricane Center Tropical Cyclone Reports and HURDAT2 best-track data for the 2027 season

A way it can turn out · Decided · June 1 – November 30, 2027

At least one hurricane that makes landfall on, or passes within 50 nautical miles of, the main island of Puerto Rico between June 1 and November 30, 2027 is at Category 3 or higher (maximum sustained winds of 96 kt or more) at landfall or closest approach, per NHC best-track data.[1c]

Shown byNOAA National Hurricane Center Tropical Cyclone Reports and HURDAT2 best-track data

One or more hurricanes make landfall on, or pass within 50 nautical miles of, the main island of Puerto Rico between June 1 and November 30, 2027, and every such hurricane is at Category 1 or 2 (maximum sustained winds of 64–95 kt) at landfall or closest approach, per NHC best-track data.[1d]

Appendix · what the memo rests on

A Findings recorded on the case

inference

SJU's record splits a hurricane strike by intensity. A catastrophic Category 4 landfall (Maria, Sep 20, 2017) cut SJU domestic enplanements 12.2% over the following 12 months, with months down as much as 31%. A Category 1 landfall (Fiona, Sep 18, 2022) closed the airport for about two days with no visible effect on monthly traffic against trend. The case's ways therefore separate Category 3+ from Category 1–2 strikes.[1c][1d][C1][C2]
