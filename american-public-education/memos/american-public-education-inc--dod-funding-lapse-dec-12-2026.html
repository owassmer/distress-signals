02 The company, and how the event reaches its revenue

The company and the revenue

For-profit postsecondary education holding company. Through its wholly owned subsidiary American Public University System (one HLC-accredited institution since August 4, 2026), it earns tuition from about 106,600 students in two segments: Military+ (online AMU/APU, paid largely by DoD/DoW Tuition Assistance, VA education benefits and Title IV aid) and Health+ (Rasmussen University and Hondros College of Nursing campus and online nursing and health programs, paid largely by Title IV aid).

The revenue this memo is about: Military+ segment tuition revenue from active-duty students paying with DoD (Department of War) Tuition Assistance, mainly at American Military University and American Public University

Owners and counterparties: Parent of American Public University System, Inc., which since the March 2, 2026 legal merger and August 4, 2026 institutional combination operates AMU, APU, Rasmussen University and Hondros College of Nursing as one institution. Graduate School USA was sold in July 2025.

How the event reaches the revenue

Tuition Assistance is funded each year by the Defense appropriations act, through the services' operation and maintenance accounts. In a lapse in DoD appropriations the services suspend TA, cancelling or withholding approvals for course starts during the lapse, so active-duty students cannot register with TA, and some cannot stay registered. APUS courses start on the first Monday of each month and run 8 (or 16) weeks, with revenue recognized over the course, so each blocked start date removes most of that cohort's TA revenue. In October-November 2025 the services bridged part of the lapse with the one-time $100M OBBBA TA appropriation, recovering about 5,000 November 2025 APUS registrations. DoD's allocation plan put all of that money in FY2026, and APEI says a significant portion may have been obligated or spent, so no such bridge is assumed for a lapse beginning December 12, 2026. VA benefits and Title IV aid are not stopped by a DoD lapse, and Health+ is not reached.

When

P.L. 119-103's continuing appropriations expire at the end of December 11, 2026. As of November 20, 2026 no further continuing resolution or Defense appropriations act had been enacted. A lapse would begin December 12, after the December 7 cohort has started, so Q4 2026 revenue is largely untouched. The effect reaches revenue through the January 4, February 1 and March 1, 2027 course starts that fall in the lapse. How far it reaches is set by the date DoD appropriations are restored, which also decides whether students' re-registration after funding returns falls inside Q1 2027.

What establishes this

  • A DoD lapse suspends TA and blocks TA-funded registrations at APUS (Military+).[7c]
  • TA is funded by the annual Defense appropriations act, so a DoD lapse is what reaches TA revenue.[7a]
  • The current funding of DoD (and TA) expires at the end of December 11, 2026.[1]
  • Timing: the effect reaches revenue through the monthly course starts (Jan 4, Feb 1, Mar 1, 2027) that fall in a lapse.[7d]
  • TA pays 45% of Military+ revenue.[8a]
  • The company's own figure for what the 2025 shutdown cost Q4 2025 revenue (two blocked starts), against which the per-start loss rate is reconciled.[9a]
  • In 2025 the OBBBA TA funds bridged part of the lapse, so the 2025 loss is net of that bridge.[9b]
  • The OBBBA TA bridge is probably unavailable, or available only in part, in a December 2026 lapse.[7e]
  • DoD's allocation plan put the whole $100M OBBBA TA appropriation in FY2026, which ended September 30, 2026.[3b]
  • As of November 20, 2026 the Senate was only beginning to take up a House-passed FY2027 bill (MilCon-VA) as a possible vehicle; nothing covering DoD had been enacted.[2a]

03 The event, and the ways it can turn out

DoD funding lapses Dec 12, 2026
Decided · On or by December 12, 2026

Appropriations for the Department of Defense (styled the Department of War) lapse when the continuing appropriations of P.L. 119-103 expire at the end of December 11, 2026: neither a full-year FY2027 Defense appropriations act nor a further continuing resolution covering DoD is enacted by then, so DoD begins operating under a lapse in appropriations on December 12, 2026.

Decided by Congress and the President in the lame-duck session of the 119th Congress. Any bill needs 60 votes in the Senate, so at least seven Democrats. Bearing on it: P.L. 119-103 funds agencies only through December 11, 2026, and no FY2027 appropriations bill has become law. On November 20, 2026 the Senate began on the motion to proceed to H.R. 8469, the House-passed FY2027 Military Construction-VA bill (passed 400-15), a possible vehicle. MilCon-VA by itself does not fund the DoD operation and maintenance accounts that pay Tuition Assistance, so it keeps DoD from lapsing only if a continuing resolution or the Defense bill is attached. Analysts expect another stopgap into 2027. Some Senate Democrats say any short-term package must restrict funds for the war against Iran, and the Defense bill (over $1 trillion topline) is among the thorniest. The 119th Congress has already had a 43-day full shutdown (Oct 1 - Nov 12, 2025), a 4-day partial lapse that included Defense (Jan 31 - Feb 3, 2026) and a 76-day DHS lapse. Democrats take majorities in both chambers on January 3, 2027. Nov 20, 2026: Senate began consideration of the motion to proceed to H.R. 8469 (FY2027 MilCon-VA), a possible vehicle (record). Early December 2026: Congress returns from Thanksgiving recess (approximate). Dec 11, 2026, end of day: P.L. 119-103 continuing appropriations expire (statutory, firm). Dec 12, 2026: a lapse begins if nothing is enacted (firm). Jan 3, 2027: 120th Congress convenes (firm).[1][2a][2b][3a][4a][4b][4c][5][6a][7a]

Appendix · what the memo rests on

A Findings recorded on the case

inference

The one-time OBBBA Tuition Assistance money that bridged part of the 2025 lapse is probably not available in meaningful amount for a DoD lapse beginning December 12, 2026. That money is the $100M in P.L. 119-21 Sec. 20001. (1) Legally, it remains available for obligation through September 30, 2029, if not earlier exhausted. (2) DoD's FY2026 Mandatory Funding Allocation Plan placed the whole $100M in FY2026, which ended September 30, 2026: Army $30M, Navy $25M, Marine Corps $15M, Air Force $30M, all O&M. (3) The services drew on it from late October 2025. The Navy restored TA for classes starting through December 31, 2025, and all four services used it for November 2025 starts, which recovered about 5,000 APUS registrations (roughly $3.75M of APUS tuition at $750 a course). The Army also used it for retroactive October 2025 TA requests, and apparently to keep active-duty TA running through the January 31 - February 3, 2026 lapse, when only non-activated reservists lost approvals. (4) APEI's 10-K (March 12, 2026) says a significant portion may have been obligated or spent and may be available 'if at all on a limited basis' in another shutdown. None of APEI's three 2026 10-Qs mentions the OBBBA TA funds again. No public record found gives the remaining balance. The case therefore assumes no OBBBA bridge. If a balance remains and the services use it as in 2025, the per-start loss falls from about 0.67 back toward the 0.52 implied by the company's 2025 figure, about $2M less per blocked start. Signs of a remaining balance would be service announcements during a lapse that OBBBA funds are being used, or DoD's FY2027 budget justification for the P.L. 119-21 funds.[7e][7k][3b][3a][7l][9b][13][9g][C1]

inference

Case case_055e775a25d2 models only a DoD lapse beginning December 12, 2026, when P.L. 119-103 expires. Reporting expects the lame-duck Congress to pass another stopgap into 2027 rather than full-year bills. As of November 20, 2026 the Senate had only begun on the motion to proceed to H.R. 8469 (FY2027 MilCon-VA), a possible vehicle. If that stopgap expires in February or March 2027, when Democrats hold majorities in both chambers of the 120th Congress, a second DoD lapse could begin then and block the Feb 1 or Mar 1, 2027 APUS starts, or Q2 starts. That route sits in this case's 'Not: DoD funding lapses Dec 12, 2026' world, which is held at baseline, so the case understates Q1-Q2 2027 TA exposure if the December stopgap is short. The revised per-start arithmetic, reconciled with the company's $12-15M estimate for the 2025 shutdown and with no OBBBA bridge, is about 0.328 x 0.67 = 22% of a quarter's TA revenue (about $8.8M) per blocked monthly start when the next start's rebound falls partly inside the quarter. It is about 0.328 x 0.76 = 25% (about $10M) when no rebound falls inside the quarter. The new stopgap's expiry date should be made its own proposition and case once it is enacted.[4a][4d][2a][C1]

WITH f AS (
  SELECT 12000000.0 AS short_lo, 15000000.0 AS short_hi, 2.0 AS blocked_starts,
         5000.0 AS obbba_regs, 250.0*3 AS ta_per_course,
         82400000.0 AS q4_2024_apus_rev, 0.46 AS ta_share_2024, 3.01 AS q4_cohort_eq,
         89400000.0 AS mil_rev_q1_2026, 0.45 AS ta_share_q1_2026, 0.328 AS cohort_share,
         97100.0 AS regs_q4_2024, 82200.0 AS regs_q4_2025, 20600.0 AS ta_drop, 0.11 AS nonta_growth,
         0.41 AS dec_ta_growth, 0.04 AS underlying_growth, 31.0/56.0 AS dec_in_q4,
         319800000.0 AS apus_rev_2025, 0.41 AS ta_share_2025, 248814000.0 AS mil_rev_9m_2025, 0.43 AS ta_share_9m_2025
), b AS (
  SELECT f.*,
    q4_2024_apus_rev*ta_share_2024/q4_cohort_eq AS base_q4_2024_per_cohort,
    mil_rev_q1_2026*ta_share_q1_2026*cohort_share AS base_q1_2026_per_cohort,
    regs_q4_2024 - (regs_q4_2025 - regs_q4_2024 + ta_drop)/nonta_growth AS ta_regs_q4_2024
  FROM f
), c AS (
  SELECT b.*,
    (base_q4_2024_per_cohort + base_q1_2026_per_cohort)/2 AS base_per_cohort,
    obbba_regs*ta_per_course AS obbba_rev_saved,
    (dec_ta_growth - underlying_growth)*(ta_regs_q4_2024/3.0) AS rebound_excess_regs
  FROM b
), d AS (
  SELECT c.*,
    rebound_excess_regs*ta_per_course*dec_in_q4 AS rebound_rev_in_q4,
    apus_rev_2025*ta_share_2025 - mil_rev_9m_2025*ta_share_9m_2025 AS ta_rev_q4_2025_derived,
    q4_2024_apus_rev*ta_share_2024*(1+underlying_growth) AS ta_rev_q4_2025_counterfactual
  FROM c
)
SELECT
  ROUND(base_q4_2024_per_cohort) AS base_q4_2024_per_cohort,
  ROUND(base_q1_2026_per_cohort) AS base_q1_2026_per_cohort,
  ROUND(base_per_cohort) AS base_per_cohort,
  ROUND(short_lo/blocked_starts) AS loss_per_start_2025_lo,
  ROUND(short_hi/blocked_starts) AS loss_per_start_2025_hi,
  ROUND(short_lo/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_2025_with_obbba_lo,
  ROUND(short_hi/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_2025_with_obbba_hi,
  ROUND((short_lo+short_hi)/2/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_2025_with_obbba_mid,
  ROUND(obbba_rev_saved) AS obbba_rev_saved,
  ROUND((short_lo+obbba_rev_saved)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_no_obbba_lo,
  ROUND((short_hi+obbba_rev_saved)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_no_obbba_hi,
  ROUND(((short_lo+short_hi)/2+obbba_rev_saved)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_no_obbba_mid,
  ROUND(ta_regs_q4_2024) AS ta_regs_q4_2024,
  ROUND(rebound_excess_regs) AS rebound_excess_regs,
  ROUND(rebound_rev_in_q4) AS rebound_rev_in_q4,
  ROUND(rebound_rev_in_q4/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rebound_partial_per_start,
  ROUND(((short_lo+short_hi)/2+obbba_rev_saved+rebound_rev_in_q4)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_no_obbba_no_rebound,
  ROUND(((short_lo+short_hi)/2+obbba_rev_saved+rebound_rev_in_q4)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort
        - (rebound_rev_in_q4/dec_in_q4)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_no_obbba_full_rebound,
  ROUND(ta_rev_q4_2025_derived) AS ta_rev_q4_2025_derived,
  ROUND(ta_rev_q4_2025_counterfactual) AS ta_rev_q4_2025_counterfactual,
  ROUND(ta_rev_q4_2025_counterfactual - ta_rev_q4_2025_derived) AS ta_shortfall_q4_2025_mix,
  ROUND((ta_rev_q4_2025_counterfactual - ta_rev_q4_2025_derived)/blocked_starts/base_per_cohort,3) AS rate_2025_from_ta_mix
FROM d
