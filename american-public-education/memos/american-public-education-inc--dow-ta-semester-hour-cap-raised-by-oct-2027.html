02 The company, and how the event reaches its revenue

The company and the revenue

For-profit postsecondary education holding company. Through its wholly owned subsidiary American Public University System (one HLC-accredited institution since August 4, 2026), it earns tuition from about 106,600 students in two segments: Military+ (online AMU/APU, paid largely by DoD/DoW Tuition Assistance, VA education benefits and Title IV aid) and Health+ (Rasmussen University and Hondros College of Nursing campus and online nursing and health programs, paid largely by Title IV aid).

The revenue this memo is about: Military+ segment tuition revenue from active-duty students paying with DoD (Department of War) Tuition Assistance, mainly at American Military University and American Public University

Owners and counterparties: Parent of American Public University System, Inc., which since the March 2, 2026 legal merger and August 4, 2026 institutional combination operates AMU, APU, Rasmussen University and Hondros College of Nursing as one institution. Graduate School USA was sold in July 2025.

How the event reaches the revenue

APUS prices its active-duty courses at the TA limit. Its Preferred Military Rate is $250 per credit hour for undergraduate and master's courses, against standard rates of $360 and $470, and management says the $250 per credit hour TA limit dictates the price it charges undergraduate active-duty students. If DoW raises the per-semester-hour TA limit, APUS can lift its TA-funded price toward the new limit without the students paying out of pocket, raising revenue per TA course. How much carries through is limited by the unchanged $4,500 annual TA maximum, by whether the services adopt the full $350, and by APUS's pricing choice.

When

Final FY2027 NDAA passage, expected in the December 2026 lame-duck session or later, would precede DoD regulations, which neither bill text sets a deadline for. The effect starts with the first monthly APUS course start (first Monday of the month) after the new limit takes effect, and is recognized over each course's 8 weeks, so 2027 revenue depends on the months remaining after the effective date.

What establishes this

  • APEI identifies the TA reimbursement limit in the pending NDAA as bearing on its revenue.[3b]
  • The TA per-credit limit sets APUS's price to active-duty undergraduates, so a higher limit reaches revenue per course.[4b]
  • APUS's military price equals the current $250 TA limit for undergraduate and master's courses.[6a]
  • The House-passed FY2027 NDAA would require DoD to raise the TA limit to $350 per semester hour.[1]

03 The event, and the ways it can turn out

DoW raises TA cap above $250/semester hour by Oct 1, 2027
Decided · November 23, 2026 – October 1, 2027

The Department of War (Department of Defense) raises the DoD-wide military Tuition Assistance limit above $250 per semester hour, by regulation, instruction or policy for the military services, effective for courses starting on or before October 1, 2027.

Decided by the Secretary of War (Defense), through Personnel and Readiness, most likely after the FY2027 NDAA is enacted. The House-passed NDAA (H.R. 8800) says the Secretary shall prescribe regulations raising the TA per-semester-hour limit to $350; the Senate committee bill (S. 4784) says the Secretary may raise it to not more than $350; neither is enacted. APEI's CEO said after the November 2026 elections that the NDAA is being reconsidered, possibly at a lower total. DoW is separately evaluating TA changes that could raise or lower reimbursement by new program classifications. A higher limit raises TA cost per credit, which Defense appropriations would have to fund. December 2026: possible final FY2027 NDAA passage in the lame-duck session, or a slip into the 120th Congress convening January 3, 2027 (uncertain). DoD regulations follow enactment; neither text sets a deadline (uncertain). The effective date may align with a fiscal-year start, October 1, 2027, or with the policy's issuance (uncertain).[1][2][3a][4a][5]

A Findings recorded on the case

inference

Case case_4abcd1984833 models only the upside of a higher TA per-semester-hour limit. DoW is also evaluating classification-based reimbursement that could lower TA rates for some programs, and APEI says it has no information on what DoW will do, so a downside proposition should be added if DoW publishes a proposal. Neither NDAA text sets a deadline for DoD's regulations, and neither changes the $4,500 annual maximum, so the timing ways and the 0.5 pass-through rest on assumptions. The case's 2027 upside is about $10.1M with the limit effective April-October 2027 and $21.4M with it effective by April 1, 2027, against a TA base of about $151M; on a full year it would be about $30M (0.2 x $151M).[4d][3a]
